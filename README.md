# Avery's World

A colorful, offline iPhone and iPad app for little kids (about ages 2–6), built with SwiftUI. Coco, a silly crocodile, hosts every game. Tap her for a joke.

## Games

The home screen has three big choices: **Learn** (Read, Math, Español, ABC, Trace, Spell, 1 2 3, Piano), **Play** (Nail Salon,
Hair Salon, Dress Up, Dentist, Mochi, Maze, Coloring, Draw) and **Team Up**, games to play with a grown-up (Bingo, Tic-Tac-Toe, Memory, Routines).

| | Game | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 💅 | **Nail Salon** | A big open hand with long nails that reach past the fingertips. Tap a nail to zoom in, then paint it with a brush by dragging a finger; brushing a new color over another blends them. Polish comes in plain colors and fine glitter, some fading from one color into another. Add dots, stripes, hearts or stars, stick flowers, gems and more right where you tap (up to five on a nail), then tap Done to show off. A "Copy this!" card shows a look to try (tap it for another), and a button changes the skin tone. |
| 💇 | **Hair Salon** | Shampoo, rinse, blow dry, comb and cut the hair of a girl drawn like a fashion doll (big eyes, lashes, glossy lips), by dragging each tool over it. Then pick a style (ponytail, pigtails, space buns, afro puff, curly), a hair color and a bow. Five friends (Mia, Lily, Zoe, Rosa and Avery) take turns, with the ones waiting shown under Up next; when she's done, take a picture (a camera flash and an instant photo) or call the next friend. |
| 👗 | **Dress Up** | Dress a fashion doll with long glossy hair, long legs and a hand-on-hip pose: a sparkly gown, party dress, tutu, t-shirt, overalls or pajamas in any color (hot pink first), plus high heels or other shoes, a hat, and a purse, wand, sunglasses or necklace. Tap Done for a fashion show, then take a picture or call the next friend: the same five friends take turns modeling. |
| 🔍 | **Spot It** | Spot the difference in a living room, a bedroom, the park or a classroom (they take turns), drawn soft and flat like a cartoon episode (Art/spot.js). In the second picture things go missing, change color, move, turn around or grow. Pick Easy (3 of the biggest things, missing or recolored), Medium (5) or Hard (7 small details). Tap a difference in either picture and it gets circled in both; a wrong tap just shows a little x. |
| 🧩 | **Puzzles** | Jigsaw puzzles of silly Coco pictures (in the bath with a duck on her head, at a party in a top hat and sunglasses, biting a giant pizza, floating off on balloons, riding Mochi in a crown, at ballet, at the beach in a sunhat, surfing on the school bus roof; drawn by Art/puzzles.js) with real knobbed pieces, 9 to start (or 4 or 12), scattered around a faint copy of the picture. Drag a piece near its place and it snaps in; let go anywhere else and it stays there, like a real puzzle. Puzzle play at this age goes with better spatial skills later (Susan Levine's lab, University of Chicago). |
| 🦷 | **Dentist** | An animal patient opens wide at the dentist. Brush the dirty teeth until they shine, floss out the bits of food, tap the teeth with holes to fix them, then rinse with mouthwash. Four animals take turns (Leo the lion, Bobo the bear, Bella the bunny and Kiki the kitty), and each gets a smiling photo at the end. The tools are drawn big, with the bristles or floss right under your finger, and wiggle as you scrub. The tool to use next bounces. |
| 🐶 | **Mochi** | Mochi, a Shiba Inu puppy, trots around her living room. Throw her ball and she runs to fetch it; put down her bowl and she walks over and eats; spray her clean with the hose (the mud fades, then she shakes off); tap Sleep and she curls up in her bed; rub her for hearts. Her food, love, energy and clean meters slowly drop while the app is closed, like a Tamagotchi, but never to zero. |
| 🫘 | **Coco Hop** | Climbing a beanstalk, choosing the way: each step up has two or three leaves on little branches, and only one holds a magic bean. Whichever she taps, Coco crouches, springs up (sometimes with a flip), and lands with a squish; on a bean leaf she wiggles, picks it up and Bella counts it into the tray of ten. Ten steps reach the cloud, where Coco spins and says how many beans she got. Every leaf is a hop up, so there's no way to lose. |
| 🎹 | **Piano** | A rainbow keyboard. Pick a song (Twinkle Twinkle, Mary Had a Little Lamb, Hot Cross Buns, Row Your Boat, Old MacDonald, London Bridge, Wheels on the Bus, Itsy Bitsy Spider) and a bouncing star shows the next key while the words light up, or tap Listen and the piano plays it by itself, each key going down with its word lit, then it's her turn. Or play freely. Slide a finger across the keys to glide up and down. |
| 📖 | **Read** | A shelf of storybooks: The Three Little Pigs, Goldilocks, The Tortoise and the Hare, The Little Red Hen, The Gingerbread Man, Little Red Riding Hood, The Lion and the Mouse, Jack and the Beanstalk, and The Boy Who Cried Wolf. Bella reads them. Slide a finger under the words and each word is read aloud as the finger reaches it, so she can follow along word by word; the last word stays lit. Tap the picture to hear the whole page. Read Together (the two-people button) adds a question on each page for the grown-up to ask ("What do you think will happen next?"), as in dialogic reading, the method Reach Out and Read uses. A Spanish shelf, Cuentos en español, has La Cucarachita Martina, Juan Bobo y la puerca, La Ciguapa del bosque, El Ratón Pérez, Medio Pollito and El Gallo de Bodas, read word by word in Coco's Spanish voice. The shelf also has Sound it out: tap each letter of a word like "cat" to hear its sound, then pick the matching picture. |
| 🎵 | **Rhyme Time** | Hearing the sounds in words: "Which one rhymes with cat?" (bat, sun, dog) and "Which one starts with /s/?", taking turns, three pictures to pick from. Tap the card to hear it again. |
| ❓ | **What's Next?** | Patterns: a row of pictures repeats (apple, banana, apple, banana...) and she picks what comes next. It starts with two things taking turns, then gets longer (apple, apple, banana) and adds a third thing as she earns stars. |
| 🧺 | **Sort It** | One picture at a time goes in one of two baskets: fruit or animals, big or small, in the sky or in the water, food or clothes. The sorts take turns, so the same whale is big one time and in the water the next. |
| 😊 | **Feelings** | Eleven feelings (happy, excited, loved, silly, sad, worried, scared, mad, surprised, calm, sleepy), with the faces filling the screen. Two kinds of rounds take turns: "Which one feels sad?" and a moment from her day ("Your ice cream fell on the floor! How would you feel?"). The three faces always come from clearly different feelings. Then Bella says one thing that helps (a hug for sad, a big slow breath for mad, a grown-up's hand for scared), and its picture appears on the face. |
| 🍕 | **Math** | Picture math with food, in four kinds that take turns, borrowing from "Russian math": adding ("Coco has 2 slices of pizza. Mochi brings 1 more. How many now?"), comparing ("Who has more cookies, Coco or Mochi?", or fewer), the missing part ("2 are on the plate. How many are in the box?", shown as 2 + ▢ = 5) and taking away ("Mochi eats 2. How many are left?"). Tap the food to count it, then pick the answer. Numbers up to 10. |
| 🗣️ | **Español** | Spanish words and phrases in six sets: greetings, family (talking with abuela and abuelo: "Bendición, abuela"), food (Dominican first: mangú, tostones, sancocho; in the DR a plantain is plátano and a banana is guineo), animals, colors and numbers. Tap a card to hear the Spanish, then the English in Bella's voice. |
| 🔤 | **ABC** | Tap a letter to see and hear it: "A is for Apple!" |
| ✏️ | **Trace** | Trace capital letters with a finger along dotted paths on writing paper: any part in any order, and close to the line counts (green dots hint where each part starts); ABC goes through the alphabet and My name traces A-V-E-R-Y. |
| 🔢 | **1 2 3** | Pick a number, then tap each item to count out loud. |
| 🎨 | **Coloring** | A coloring book: a flower, a house, a fish, an ice cream cone and a butterfly. Pick a crayon (Coco says its name), then tap a part to fill it or color it in with a finger. |
| 🖍️ | **Draw** | Draw with your finger in lines that look like real crayon, with a waxy edge and paper showing through. Twelve soft crayon colors down the side (Coco names each one), plus undo and clear. |
| 🎲 | **Bingo** | Picture bingo for a kid and a grown-up together, against Coco, like Zingo: slide out two tiles (Coco reads their words), drag one onto the same picture on your card to cover it, and fill your card before Coco does. Coco is a little slow, on purpose. |
| 🔢 | **Number Race** | A board game for a kid and a grown-up: a straight row of squares numbered 1 to 10. Spin 1 or 2, then tap each square you hop onto while Bella says its number; first to 10 wins. Robert Siegler and Geetha Ramani found that a few short games like this made preschoolers much better at judging how big numbers are. |
| ⭐️ | **Tic-Tac-Toe** | For a kid and a grown-up on one iPad: stars against hearts, three in a row wins. The winning row lights up and each side's wins are counted. |
| 🃏 | **Memory** | For a kid and a grown-up taking turns: turn over two cards to find matching animals. A match is yours and you go again; otherwise the cards flip back and it's the other side's turn. |
| 🔎 | **I Spy** | Coco gives a clue in one of the Spot It rooms ("I spy something that tells the time") and Stars and Hearts take turns finding it. Found things get circled; when the room's done, the next room comes up. |
| 🌙 | **Routines** | Morning and bedtime checklists to use with a grown-up: big picture buttons for each step (restroom, vitamins, get dressed, breakfast... or bath, pajamas, restroom, story, lights out). The child taps each one as it's done, and the next one bounces. A red Hurry timer (5 to 20 minutes) for when she needs a nudge shows the sand running out, and Coco says when time's up. Order lets the grown-up drag the steps into the family's own order, saved on the device. |
| 🧹 | **Chores** | The same checklist for helping at home: make your bed, put toys away, clothes in the hamper, feed the pet, set the table, water the plants, sweep and wipe the table, with the Hurry timer and the grown-up's own order. |

The first time the app opens, a grown-up types the child's name (the pencil by the title on the home screen changes it). It's used on the home screen ("Liam's World"), in Trace's My name, on the photos, as the fifth salon friend, at bedtime and in Español. Lines with the name are recorded for Avery; for other names Coco says them without it ("Hi!"), and "Me llamo…" uses the iPad's Spanish voice. Pictures taken in Hair Salon, Dress Up, Dentist, Nail Salon, Coloring and Draw are saved to the iPad's Photos (iOS asks the first time). Wins in any game add up to stickers for the **Sticker book** on the home screen (50 to find): two wins for each of the first ten stickers, then one more per sticker for every ten in the book, about 200 wins in all. Each place has its own soft background tune, made in the app like the sound effects (`Support/Music.swift`); the music button at the top right of every game turns it off.

## Notes for parents

- **No ads, no in-app purchases, no accounts, no internet.** Nothing is collected or sent anywhere.
- Words are read aloud with the iPhone's built-in voice, so no recordings are needed. Speech still plays
  when the silent switch is on.
- For a more natural voice, download a Premium or Enhanced one: Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content →
  Voices → English (and Spanish for the Spanish games). The app uses the best voice it finds.
- Big buttons, haptic "taps", and no text you need to read to get around.
- Follows Apple's design guidelines for kids' apps: buttons at least 44 points, standard back navigation, and
  Reduce Motion (Settings → Accessibility → Motion) stills the wagging, twinkling and spinning.
- The app opens with Coco getting ready (about 2 seconds; tap to skip).
- Tip: turn on **Guided Access** (Settings → Accessibility → Guided Access) to keep your child inside the app.

## Building and running

You need a Mac with **Xcode 16 or newer**. The app runs on iOS 17 or newer.

The Xcode project is generated from `project.yml` with [XcodeGen](https://github.com/yonaskolb/XcodeGen):

```sh
brew install xcodegen
xcodegen            # creates AverysPlayground.xcodeproj
open AverysPlayground.xcodeproj
```

Then in Xcode:

1. Select the **AverysPlayground** target → **Signing & Capabilities** → choose your **Team**
   (a free Apple ID works for running on your own phone).
2. The bundle ID is `com.tejadar1.averysplayground`. If Xcode says it's taken, change it in `project.yml`.
3. Plug in the iPhone (or pick a simulator) and press **Run** (⌘R).

## TestFlight (no Mac needed)

GitHub builds the app on a Mac (`.github/workflows/build.yml`), which is costly on a private repo, so it only
builds when started by hand: Actions → Build → **Run workflow**. Each run tests the app on an iPhone simulator and
uploads it to TestFlight. Screenshots of every game on iPhone and iPad take about 10 more minutes, so they only
run when you tick **Take screenshots**. The upload
needs these one-time steps, all doable in Safari on an iPhone.

1. Join the [Apple Developer Program](https://developer.apple.com/programs/) ($99/year).
2. On [developer.apple.com](https://developer.apple.com/account/resources/identifiers/list), add an
   **Identifier** → App IDs → App, with the explicit bundle ID `com.tejadar1.averysplayground`.
3. In [App Store Connect](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com/apps), tap **+ New App**: iOS, pick that bundle ID,
   any SKU. The name must be unused on the App Store, so use something like "Avery's World: Kids Games".
   The name under the icon is "Avery's World" either way (set in `project.yml`).
4. In App Store Connect → Users and Access → Integrations → **App Store Connect API**, generate a team key
   with **Admin** access and download the `.p8` file (Apple lets you download it only once).
5. Put the key's Key ID, the Issuer ID and your Team ID (Membership details on
   [developer.apple.com/account](https://developer.apple.com/account)) in the `testflight` job in
   `.github/workflows/build.yml`. They aren't secret.
6. In this GitHub repo → Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions, add one repository secret,
   `APP_STORE_CONNECT_KEY`: the full text of the `.p8` file, including the BEGIN and END lines.
7. In GitHub → Actions → Build → **Run workflow**. When it finishes, Apple takes 10–30 minutes to process the
   build. Then in App Store Connect → your app → TestFlight → Internal Testing, add yourself as a tester and
   install it with the [TestFlight](https://apps.apple.com/app/testflight/id899247664) app.

After that, each run uploads a new build, and TestFlight offers the update.

## Project layout

```
AverysPlayground/
  App/          App entry point, home screen, list of games
  Activities/   One SwiftUI view per game
  Support/      Speech, haptics, fonts and shared styles
  Assets.xcassets
AverysPlaygroundTests/    Unit tests (run with ⌘U)
AverysPlaygroundUITests/  Opens every game and saves screenshots to the `screenshots` branch (manual builds with screenshots ticked)
Art/            SVG sources for Coco, Mochi, the game icons (Art/icons), the pictures in the games (Art/pictures), the app icon and launch screen
project.yml     XcodeGen project definition
```

### Art

The art is soft cartoon color with a thin dark-brown outline (`Color.outline` in code), a darker second tone for shading, and small dot eyes.
Coco and Mochi are SVGs in `Art/` (Mochi's room is `mochi-room.svg`, and `mochi.svg` holds her side view in parts that move). The pictures in the games (Bingo tiles, ABC, stories and so on) are in `Art/pictures`, and the code shows one with `Picture("cat", size:)`; the app uses no emoji. The Dress Up doll (`doll.svg`) and the hair salon customer's face (`salon.svg`) are drawn in a softer style, one layer per part, written by `node Art/doll.js`: edit `doll.js`, not the SVGs. Their white and gray parts get tinted in the app with the friend's skin, hair and eye colors and the outfit color; the salon customer's hair is drawn in code so it can be cut. After editing an SVG, re-render the PNGs into the asset catalog:

```sh
node Art/render.js   # needs Playwright with Chromium
```

The Nail Salon hand is drawn in code (`Hand` in `NailSalonView.swift`) so its skin tone can change.

### Coco's voice

Coco's lines are recorded with [Kokoro](https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro), a free open-source voice, with the
pitch raised for a cartoon sound, so she doesn't sound like the iPad's built-in voice. Spanish (the Spanish cards and
stories) uses Kokoro's Spanish voice the same way. `Voice/lines.txt` lists every line, Spanish ones starting with
`es:` (made by `python3 Voice/lines.py > Voice/lines.txt`), and `Voice/voice.txt` names the voice and pitch for
Coco, Spanish, Bella (the storyteller and teacher) and the friends (Sky, a young girl's voice), one per line. Run
Actions → Build → Run workflow with **voice: record** to record new lines into `AverysPlayground/Voice/` (free, on
Linux), or **voice: samples** to hear several voices on the `voice-samples` branch. Lines without a recording use the iPad's
voice.

### Building with Claude Code

Two tools from GitHub are part of how this app gets built. Claude Code loads both when it opens this repo:

- [ponytail](https://github.com/DietrichGebert/ponytail) keeps code changes small: reuse what's in the
  project, skip features nobody asked for, and add a test for new logic. It's a plugin, turned on in
  `.claude/settings.json`; Claude Code offers to install it the first time you trust the folder. Switch
  levels with `/ponytail lite`, `/ponytail ultra`, or turn it off with `stop ponytail`.
- [no-ai-slop](https://github.com/petergyang/no-ai-slop) keeps writing plain: the README, text kids see
  in the app, and commit messages. It has no Claude Code plugin, so a copy of the skill lives in
  `.claude/skills/no-ai-slop` (MIT licensed). Run it on any text with `/no-ai-slop`.

To get both on your own machine for every project, run these in Claude Code:

```
/plugin marketplace add DietrichGebert/ponytail
/plugin install ponytail@ponytail
```

and in a terminal: `npx skills add petergyang/no-ai-slop --skill no-ai-slop --global --yes`.

### Adding a new game

1. Create a view in `AverysPlayground/Activities/`.
2. Add a case to `Activity` in `App/Activity.swift` with a title, color, and destination.
3. Draw its icon as `Art/icons/<case name>.svg` and run `node Art/render.js`.

It shows up on the home screen automatically.
